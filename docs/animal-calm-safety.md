# Animal Calm safety design

Animal Calm is an educational environment-planning feature. It is not a veterinary treatment, diagnosis tool, frequency prescription, or claim that a particular sound will calm an animal.

## Product boundaries

- Never place headphones, earbuds, wearable speakers, or vibration devices on an animal.
- The Web Audio tone player is designed for a consenting human listener and is not connected to Animal Calm.
- Animal Calm produces no sound. Its five-minute timer is an observation aid only.
- Caregivers are instructed to begin with silence, keep speakers distant and extremely low when ambient masking sound is used, and preserve a clear retreat or exit.
- Species profiles emphasize observable behavior rather than a universal preset.
- Sudden hearing changes, ear pain, balance problems, breathing changes, appetite changes, or other concerning signs are directed to veterinary care.

## Why the feature avoids animal frequency presets

Dogs and cats can detect higher frequencies and are more sensitive to sound than people. Noise trauma can contribute to acquired hearing loss in animals. Individual animals, species, age groups, and health conditions also differ, so a human comfort level is not an animal safety threshold.

Animal Calm therefore does not map species, symptoms, body systems, or conditions to carrier frequencies. It supports quiet-room preparation and careful observation instead.

## Review rule for future changes

Any future animal-audio feature must receive a separate safety review. It must not inherit human listening defaults, binaural offsets, headphone guidance, or frequency-tradition claims.
